abstract: Neural Processes (NPs) are deep probabilistic models that represent stochastic
  processes by conditioning their prior distributions on a set of context points.
  Despite their advantages in uncertainty estimation for complex distributions, NPs
  enforce parameterization coupling between the conditional prior model and the posterior
  model. We show that this coupling amounts to prior misspecification and revisit
  the NP objective to address this issue. More specifically, we propose Rényi Neural
  Processes (RNP), a method that replaces the standard KL divergence with the Rényi
  divergence, dampening the effects of the misspecified prior during posterior updates.
  We validate our approach across multiple benchmarks including regression and image
  inpainting tasks, and show significant performance improvements of RNPs in real-world
  problems. Our extensive experiments show consistently better log-likelihoods over
  state-of-the-art NP models.
