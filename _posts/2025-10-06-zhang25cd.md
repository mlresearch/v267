title: 'e-GAI: e-value-based Generalized $α$-Investing for Online False Discovery
  Rate Control'
openreview: Pedm1880A2
abstract: Online multiple hypothesis testing has attracted a lot of attention in many
  applications, e.g., anomaly status detection and stock market price monitoring.
  The state-of-the-art generalized $\alpha$-investing (GAI) algorithms can control
  online false discovery rate (FDR) on p-values only under specific dependence structures,
  a situation that rarely occurs in practice. The e-LOND algorithm (Xu & Ramdas, 2024)
  utilizes e-values to achieve online FDR control under arbitrary dependence but suffers
  from a significant loss in power as testing levels are derived from pre-specified
  descent sequences. To address these limitations, we propose a novel framework on
  valid e-values named e-GAI. The proposed e-GAI can ensure provable online FDR control
  under more general dependency conditions while improving the power by dynamically
  allocating the testing levels. These testing levels are updated not only by relying
  on both the number of previous rejections and the prior costs, but also, differing
  from the GAI framework, by assigning less $\alpha$-wealth for each rejection from
  a risk aversion perspective. Within the e-GAI framework, we introduce two new online
  FDR procedures, e-LORD and e-SAFFRON, and provide strategies for the long-term performance
  to address the issue of $\alpha$-death, a common phenomenon within the GAI framework.
  Furthermore, we demonstrate that e-GAI can be generalized to conditionally super-uniform
  p-values. Both simulated and real data experiments demonstrate the advantages of
  both e-LORD and e-SAFFRON in FDR control and power.
