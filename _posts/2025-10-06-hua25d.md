title: 'OpenworldAUC: Towards Unified Evaluation and Optimization for Open-world Prompt
  Tuning'
openreview: BiyGtuM20U
abstract: 'Prompt tuning adapts Vision-Language Models like CLIP to open-world tasks
  with minimal training costs. In this direction, one typical paradigm evaluates model
  performance <b>separately</b> on known classes (<em>i.e.</em>, base domain) and
  unseen classes (<em>i.e.</em>, new domain). However, real-world scenarios require
  models to handle inputs <b>without prior domain knowledge</b>. This practical challenge
  has spurred the development of <b>open-world prompt tuning</b>, which demands a
  unified evaluation of two stages: 1) detecting whether an input belongs to the base
  or new domain (<b>P1</b>), and 2) classifying the sample into its correct class
  (<b>P2</b>). What’s more, as domain distributions are generally unknown, a proper
  metric should be insensitive to varying base/new sample ratios (<b>P3</b>). However,
  we find that current metrics, including HM, overall accuracy, and AUROC, fail to
  satisfy these three properties simultaneously. To bridge this gap, we propose $\mathsf{OpenworldAUC}$,
  a unified metric that jointly assesses detection and classification through pairwise
  instance comparisons. To optimize $\mathsf{OpenworldAUC}$ effectively, we introduce
  <b>Gated Mixture-of-Prompts (GMoP)</b>, which employs domain-specific prompts and
  a gating mechanism to dynamically balance detection and classification. Theoretical
  guarantees ensure generalization of GMoP under practical conditions. Experiments
  on 15 benchmarks in open-world scenarios show GMoP achieves SOTA performance on
  $\mathsf{OpenworldAUC}$ and other metrics.'
