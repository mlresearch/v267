  Depth, and Hyperparameter Transfer'
openreview: SEj9uopOWP
abstract: We theoretically characterize gradient descent dynamics in deep linear networks
  trained at large width from random initialization and on large quantities of random
  data. Our theory captures the “wider is better" effect of mean-field/maximum-update
  parameterized networks as well as hyperparameter transfer effects, which can be
  contrasted with the neural-tangent parameterization where optimal learning rates
  shift with model width. We provide asymptotic descriptions of both non-residual
  and residual neural networks, the latter of which enables an infinite depth limit
  when branches are scaled as $1/\sqrt{\text{depth}}$. We also compare training with
  one-pass stochastic gradient descent to the dynamics when training data are repeated
  at each iteration. Lastly, we show that this model recovers the accelerated power
  law training dynamics for power law structured data in the rich regime observed
  in recent works.
