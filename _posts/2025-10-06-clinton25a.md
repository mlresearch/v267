title: 'Collaborative Mean Estimation Among Heterogeneous Strategic Agents: Individual
  Rationality, Fairness, and Truthful Contribution'
openreview: OUr945QeUb
abstract: 'We study a collaborative learning problem where $m$ agents aim to estimate
  a vector $\mu =(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_d)\in \mathbb{R}^d$ by sampling from associated
  univariate normal distributions $(\mathcal{N}(\mu_k, \sigma^2))_{k\in[d]}$. Agent
  $i$ incurs a cost $c_{i,k}$ to sample from $\mathcal{N}(\mu_k, \sigma^2)$. Instead
  of working independently, agents can exchange data, collecting cheaper samples and
  sharing them in return for costly data, thereby reducing both costs and estimation
  error. We design a mechanism to facilitate such collaboration, while addressing
  two key challenges: ensuring <em>individually rational (IR) and fair outcomes</em>
  so all agents benefit, and <em>preventing strategic behavior</em> (e.g. non-collection,
  data fabrication) to avoid socially undesirable outcomes. We design a mechanism
  and an associated Nash equilibrium (NE) which minimizes the social penalty-sum of
  agents’ estimation errors and collection costs-while being IR for all agents. We
  achieve a $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{m})$-approximation to the minimum social penalty in
  the worst case and an $\mathcal{O}(1)$-approximation under favorable conditions.
  Additionally, we establish three hardness results: no nontrivial mechanism guarantees
  <em>(i)</em> a dominant strategy equilibrium where agents report truthfully, <em>(ii)</em>
  is IR for every strategy profile of other agents, <em>(iii)</em> or avoids a worst-case
  $\Omega(\sqrt{m})$ price of stability in any NE. Finally, by integrating concepts
  from axiomatic bargaining, we demonstrate that our mechanism supports fairer outcomes
  than one which minimizes social penalty.'
