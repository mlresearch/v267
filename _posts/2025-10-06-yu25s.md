title: 'Think Smarter not Harder: Adaptive Reasoning with Inference Aware Optimization'
openreview: 0ERw2196o1
abstract: Solving mathematics problems has been an intriguing capability of large
  language models, and many efforts have been made to improve reasoning by extending
  reasoning length, such as through self-correction and extensive long chain-of-thoughts.
  While promising in problem-solving, advanced long reasoning chain models exhibit
  an undesired single-modal behavior, where trivial questions require unnecessarily
  tedious long chains of thought. In this work, we propose a way to allow models to
  be aware of inference budgets by formulating it as utility maximization with respect
  to an inference budget constraint, hence naming our algorithm Inference Budget-Constrained
  Policy Optimization (IBPO). In a nutshell, models fine-tuned through IBPO learn
  to “understand” the difficulty of queries and allocate inference budgets to harder
  ones. With different inference budgets, our best models are able to have a $4.14$%
  and $5.74$% absolute improvement ($8.08$% and $11.2$% relative improvement) on MATH500
  using $2.16$x and $4.32$x inference budgets respectively, relative to LLaMA3.1 8B
  Instruct. These improvements are approximately $2$x those of self-consistency under
  the same budgets.
