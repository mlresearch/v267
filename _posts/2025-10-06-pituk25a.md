title: Do Bayesian Neural Networks Actually Behave Like Bayesian Models?
openreview: x5RQnF7Vw9
abstract: We empirically investigate how well popular approximate inference algorithms
  for Bayesian Neural Networks (BNNs) respect the theoretical properties of Bayesian
  belief updating. We find strong evidence on synthetic regression and real-world
  image classification tasks that common BNN algorithms such as variational inference,
  Laplace approximation, SWAG, and SGLD fail to update in a consistent manner, forget
  about old data under sequential updates, and violate the predictive coherence properties
  that would be expected of Bayesian methods. These observed behaviors imply that
  care should be taken when treating BNNs as true Bayesian models, particularly when
  using them beyond static prediction settings, such as for active, continual, or
  transfer learning.
