title: Simple Randomized Rounding for Max-Min Eigenvalue Augmentation
openreview: q4cNdrwKAk
abstract: 'We consider the <em>max-min eigenvalue augmentation</em> problem: given
  $n \times n$ symmetric positive semidefinite matrices $M,A_1,\ldots, A_m$ and a
  positive integer $k < m$, the goal is to choose a subset $I \subset \{1,\ldots,m\}$
  of cardinality at most $k$ that maximizes the minimum eigenvalue of the matrix $M
  + \sum_{i \in I} A_i$. The problem captures both the <em>Bayesian E-optimal design</em>
  and <em>maximum algebraic connectivity augmentation</em> problems. In contrast to
  the existing work, we do not assume that the <em>augmentation matrices</em> are
  rank-one matrices, and we focus on the setting in which $k < n$. We show that a
  <em>simple</em> randomized rounding method provides a constant-factor approximation
  if the <em>optimal increase</em> is sufficiently large, specifically, if $\mathrm{OPT}
  - \lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(M) = \Omega(R \ln k)$, where $\mathrm{OPT}$ is the optimal
  value, and $R$ is the maximum trace of an augmentation matrix. To establish the
  guarantee, we derive a matrix concentration inequality that is of independent interest.
  The inequality can be interpreted as an <em>intrinsic dimension</em> analog of the
  matrix Chernoff inequality for the minimum eigenvalue of a sum of independent random
  positive semidefinite matrices; such an inequality has already been established
  for the maximum eigenvalue, but not for the minimum eigenvalue.'
