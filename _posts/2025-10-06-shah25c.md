title: 'SEAD: Unsupervised Ensemble of Streaming Anomaly Detectors'

abstract: Can we efficiently choose the best Anomaly Detection (AD) algorithm for
  a data-stream without requiring anomaly labels? Streaming anomaly detection is hard.
  SOTA AD algorithms are sensitive to their hyperparameters and no single method works
  well on all datasets. The best algorithm/hyper-parameter combination for a given
  data-stream can change over time with data drift. ’What is an anomaly?’ is often
  application, context and dataset dependent. We propose SEAD (Streaming Ensemble
  of Anomaly Detectors), the first model selection algorithm for streaming, unsupervised
  AD. All prior AD model selection algorithms are either supervised, or only work
  in the offline setting when all data from the test set is available upfront. We
  show that SEAD is <em>(i)</em> unsupervised, i.e., requires no true anomaly labels,
  <em>(ii)</em> efficiently implementable in a streaming setting, <em>(iii)</em> agnostic
  to the choice of the base algorithms among which it chooses from, and <em>(iv)</em>
  adaptive to non-stationarity in the data-stream. Experiments on 14 non-trivial public
  datasets and an internal dataset corroborate our claims.
