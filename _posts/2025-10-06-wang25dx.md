abstract: In this work, we study offline reinforcement learning (RL) with zero-shot
  generalization property (ZSG), where the agent has access to an offline dataset
  including experiences from different environments, and the goal of the agent is
  to train a policy over the training environments which performs well on test environments
  without further interaction. Existing work showed that classical offline RL fails
  to generalize to new, unseen environments. We propose pessimistic empirical risk
  minimization (PERM) and pessimistic proximal policy optimization (PPPO), which leverage
  pessimistic policy evaluation to guide policy learning and enhance generalization.
  We show that both PERM and PPPO are capable of finding a near-optimal policy with
  ZSG. Our result serves as a first step in understanding the foundation of the generalization
  phenomenon in offline reinforcement learning.
