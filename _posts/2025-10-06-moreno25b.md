abstract: 'Structured Kernel Interpolation (SKI) scales Gaussian Processes (GPs) by
  approximating the kernel matrix via inducing point interpolation, achieving linear
  computational complexity. However, it lacks rigorous theoretical error analysis.
  This paper bridges this gap by proving error bounds for the SKI Gram matrix and
  examining their effect on hyperparameter estimation and posterior inference. We
  further provide a practical guide to selecting the number of inducing points under
  convolutional cubic interpolation: they should grow as $n^{d/3}$ for error control.
  Crucially, we identify two dimensionality regimes for the SKI Gram matrix spectral
  norm error vs. complexity trade-off. For $d<3$, <em>any</em> error tolerance can
  achieve linear time for sufficiently large sample size. For $d\geq 3$, the error
  must <em>increase</em> with sample size for our guarantees to hold. Our analysis
  provides key insights into SKI’s scalability-accuracy trade-offs, establishing precise
  conditions for achieving linear-time GP inference with controlled error.'
