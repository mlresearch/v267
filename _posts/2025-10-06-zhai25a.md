title: 'EquivaMap: Leveraging LLMs for Automatic Equivalence Checking of Optimization
  Formulations'
openreview: jaCD2nEpyr
abstract: A fundamental problem in combinatorial optimization is identifying equivalent
  formulations. Despite the growing need for automated equivalence checks—driven,
  for example, by <em>optimization copilots</em>, which generate problem formulations
  from natural language descriptions—current approaches rely on simple heuristics
  that fail to reliably check formulation equivalence. Inspired by Karp reductions,
  in this work we introduce <em>Quasi-Karp equivalence</em>, a formal criterion for
  determining when two optimization formulations are equivalent based on the existence
  of a mapping between their decision variables. We propose <em>EquivaMap</em>, a
  framework that leverages large language models to automatically discover such mappings
  for scalable, reliable equivalence checking, with a verification stage that ensures
  mapped solutions preserve feasibility and optimality without additional solver calls.
  To evaluate our approach, we construct <em>EquivaFormulation</em>, the first open-source
  dataset of equivalent optimization formulations, generated by applying transformations
  such as adding slack variables or valid inequalities to existing formulations. Empirically,
  <em>EquivaMap</em> significantly outperforms existing methods, achieving substantial
  improvements in correctly identifying formulation equivalence.
