abstract: Large language model (LLM) unlearning has demonstrated its essential role
  in removing privacy and copyright-related responses, crucial for their legal and
  safe applications. However, the pursuit of complete unlearning often comes with
  substantial costs due to its compromises in their general functionality, leading
  to a notorious trade-off between unlearning and retention. It motivates this paper
  to explore enhanced unlearning schemes that can mitigate this trade-off. Specifically,
  we propose Gradient Rectified Unlearning (GRU), an improved framework that regulates
  the directions of gradient updates during the unlearning procedure such that their
  side impacts on other, unrelated responses can be minimized. GRU is easy and general
  to implement, demonstrating practical effectiveness across a variety of well-established
  unlearning benchmarks.
