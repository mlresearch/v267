abstract: Large Language Models are typically trained with next-turn rewards, limiting
  their ability to optimize for long-term interaction. As a result, they often respond
  passively to ambiguous or open-ended user requests, failing to help users reach
  their ultimate intents and leading to inefficient conversations. To address these
  limitations, we introduce CollabLLM, a novel and general training framework that
  enhances multiturn human-LLM collaboration. Its key innovation is a collaborative
  simulation that estimates the long-term contribution of responses using Multiturn-aware
  Rewards. By reinforcement fine-tuning these rewards, CollabLLM goes beyond responding
  to user requests, and actively uncovers user intent and offers insightful suggestions—a
  key step towards more human-centered AI. We also devise a multiturn interaction
  benchmark with three challenging tasks such as document creation. CollabLLM significantly
  outperforms our baselines with averages of 18.5% higher task performance and 46.3%
  improved interactivity by LLM judges. Finally, we conduct a large user study with
  201 judges, where CollabLLM increases user satisfaction by 17.6% and reduces user
  spent time by 10.4%.
