abstract: 'Correlation analysis is a fundamental step in uncovering meaningful insights
  from complex datasets. In this paper, we study the problem of detecting correlations
  between two random graphs following the Gaussian Wigner model with unlabeled vertices.
  Specifically, the task is formulated as a hypothesis testing problem: under the
  null hypothesis, the two graphs are independent, while under the alternative hypothesis,
  they are edge-correlated through a latent vertex permutation, yet maintain the same
  marginal distributions as under the null. We focus on the scenario where two induced
  subgraphs, each with a fixed number of vertices, are sampled. We determine the optimal
  rate for the sample size required for correlation detection, derived through an
  analysis of the conditional second moment. Additionally, we propose an efficient
  approximate algorithm that significantly reduces running time.'
