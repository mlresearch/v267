title: 'RATE: Causal Explainability of Reward Models with Imperfect Counterfactuals'
openreview: rL3uxe5a0c
abstract: Reward models are widely used as proxies for human preferences when aligning
  or evaluating LLMs. However, reward models are black boxes, and it is often unclear
  what, exactly, they are actually rewarding. In this paper we develop Rewrite-based
  Attribute Treatment Estimator (RATE) as an effective method for measuring the sensitivity
  of a reward model to high-level attributes of responses, such as sentiment, helpfulness,
  or complexity. Importantly, RATE measures the <em>causal</em> effect of an attribute
  on the reward. RATE uses LLMs to rewrite responses to produce imperfect counterfactuals
  examples that can be used to measure causal effects. A key challenge is that these
  rewrites are imperfect in a manner that can induce substantial bias in the estimated
  sensitivity of the reward model to the attribute. The core idea of RATE is to adjust
  for this imperfect-rewrite effect by rewriting <em>twice</em>. We establish the
  validity of the RATE procedure and show empirically that it is an effective estimator.
