  Perspective'

abstract: 'Although large language models (LLMs) show promise in solving complex mathematical
  tasks, existing evaluation paradigms rely solely on a coarse measure of overall
  answer accuracy, which are insufficient for assessing their authentic capabilities.
  In this paper, we propose <b>CogMath</b>, which comprehensively assesses LLMs’ mathematical
  abilities through the lens of human cognition. Specifically, inspired by psychological
  theories, CogMath formalizes human reasoning process into 3 stages: <em>problem
  comprehension</em>, <em>problem solving</em>, and <em>solution summarization</em>.
  Within these stages, we investigate perspectives such as numerical calculation,
  knowledge, and counterfactuals, and design a total of 9 fine-grained evaluation
  dimensions. In each dimension, we develop an “<em>Inquiry</em>-<em>Judge</em>-<em>Reference</em>”
  multi-agent system to generate inquiries that assess LLMs’ mastery from this dimension.
  An LLM is considered to truly master a problem only when excelling in all inquiries
  from the 9 dimensions. By applying CogMath on three benchmarks, we reveal that the
  mathematical capabilities of 7 mainstream LLMs are overestimated by 30%-40%. Moreover,
  we locate their strengths and weaknesses across specific stages/dimensions, offering
  in-depth insights to further enhance their reasoning abilities.'
