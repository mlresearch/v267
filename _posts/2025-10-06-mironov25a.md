abstract: 'This paper addresses the problem of quantifying diversity for a set of
  objects. First, we conduct a systematic review of existing diversity measures and
  explore their undesirable behavior in certain cases. Based on this review, we formulate
  three desirable properties (axioms) of a reliable diversity measure: monotonicity,
  uniqueness, and continuity. We show that none of the existing measures has all three
  properties and thus these measures are not suitable for quantifying diversity. Then,
  we construct two examples of measures that have all the desirable properties, thus
  proving that the list of axioms is not self-contradictory. Unfortunately, the constructed
  examples are too computationally expensive (NP-hard) for practical use. Thus, we
  pose an open problem of constructing a diversity measure that has all the listed
  properties and can be computed in practice or proving that all such measures are
  NP-hard to compute.'
