abstract: Recent advances in bioengineering have enabled the creation of biological
  neural networks in vitro, significantly reducing the cost, ethical hurdles, and
  complexity of experimentation with genuine biological neural computation. In this
  position paper, we argue that this trend offers a unique and timely opportunity
  to put our understanding of neural computation to the test. By designing artificial
  neural networks that can interact and control living neural systems, it is becoming
  possible to validate computational models beyond simulation and gain empirical insights
  to help unlock more robust and energy-efficient next-generation AI systems. We provide
  an overview of key technologies, challenges, and principles behind this development
  and describe strategies and opportunities for novel machine learning research in
  this emerging field. We also discuss implications and fundamental questions that
  could be answered as this technology advances, exemplifying the longer-term impact
  of increasingly sophisticated in vitro neural networks.
