title: 'LEVIS: Large Exact Verifiable Input Spaces for Neural Networks'
openreview: WFIMSlNS7C
abstract: 'The robustness of neural networks is crucial in safety-critical applications,
  where identifying a reliable input space is essential for effective model selection,
  robustness evaluation, and the development of reliable control strategies. Most
  existing robustness verification methods assess the worst-case output under the
  assumption that the input space is known. However, precisely identifying a verifiable
  input space $ \mathcal{C} $, where no adversarial examples exist, is challenging
  due to the possible high dimensionality, discontinuity, and non-convex nature of
  the input space. To address this challenge, we propose a novel framework, <b>LEVIS</b>,
  comprising <b>LEVIS-$\alpha$</b> and <b>LEVIS-$\beta$</b>. <b>LEVIS-$\alpha$</b>
  identifies a single, large verifiable ball that intersects at least two boundaries
  of a bounded region $ \mathcal{C} $, while <b>LEVIS-$\beta$</b> systematically captures
  the entirety of the verifiable space by integrating multiple verifiable balls. Our
  contributions are fourfold: we introduce a verification framework, <b>LEVIS</b>,
  incorporating two optimization techniques for computing nearest and directional
  adversarial points based on mixed-integer programming (MIP); to enhance scalability,
  we integrate complementary constrained (CC) optimization with a reduced MIP formulation,
  achieving up to a 17-fold reduction in runtime by approximating the verifiable region
  in a principled way; we provide a theoretical analysis characterizing the properties
  of the verifiable balls obtained through <b>LEVIS-$\alpha$</b>; and we validate
  our approach across diverse applications, including electrical power flow regression
  and image classification, demonstrating performance improvements and visualizing
  the geometric properties of the verifiable region.'
