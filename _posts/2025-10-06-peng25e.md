title: 'CAT: Contrastive Adversarial Training for Evaluating the Robustness of Protective
  Perturbations in Latent Diffusion Models'
openreview: 5of0l7eUau
abstract: Latent diffusion models have recently demonstrated superior capabilities
  in many downstream image synthesis tasks. However, customization of latent diffusion
  models using unauthorized data can severely compromise the privacy and intellectual
  property rights of data owners. Adversarial examples as protective perturbations
  have been developed to defend against unauthorized data usage by introducing imperceptible
  noise to customization samples, preventing diffusion models from effectively learning
  them. In this paper, we first reveal that the primary reason adversarial examples
  are effective as protective perturbations in latent diffusion models is the distortion
  of their latent representations, as demonstrated through qualitative and quantitative
  experiments. We then propose the Contrastive Adversarial Training (CAT) utilizing
  lightweight adapters as an adaptive attack against these protection methods, highlighting
  their lack of robustness. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our CAT method
  significantly reduces the effectiveness of protective perturbations in customization,
  urging the community to reconsider and improve the robustness of existing protective
  perturbations. The code is available at https://github.com/senp98/CAT.
