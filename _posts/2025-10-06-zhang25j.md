title: When do neural networks learn world models?
openreview: oUzxhnnGVM
abstract: Humans develop <em>world models</em> that capture the underlying generation
  process of data. Whether neural networks can learn similar world models remains
  an open problem. In this work, we present the first theoretical results for this
  problem, showing that in a <em>multi-task</em> setting, models with a <em>low-degree
  bias</em> provably recover latent data-generating variables under mild assumptions–even
  if proxy tasks involve complex, non-linear functions of the latents. However, such
  recovery is sensitive to model architecture. Our analysis leverages Boolean models
  of task solutions via the Fourier-Walsh transform and introduces new techniques
  for analyzing invertible Boolean transforms, which may be of independent interest.
  We illustrate the algorithmic implications of our results and connect them to related
  research areas, including self-supervised learning, out-of-distribution generalization,
  and the linear representation hypothesis in large language models.
