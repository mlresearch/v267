abstract: Data heterogeneity across multiple sources is common in real-world machine
  learning (ML) settings. Although many methods focus on enabling a single model to
  handle diverse data, real-world markets often comprise multiple competing ML providers.
  In this paper, we propose a game-theoretic framework—the Heterogeneous Data Game—to
  analyze how such providers compete across heterogeneous data sources. We investigate
  the resulting pure Nash equilibria (PNE), showing that they can be non-existent,
  homogeneous (all providers converge on the same model), or heterogeneous (providers
  specialize in distinct data sources). Our analysis spans monopolistic, duopolistic,
  and more general markets, illustrating how factors such as the “temperature” of
  data-source choice models and the dominance of certain data sources shape equilibrium
  outcomes. We offer theoretical insights into both homogeneous and heterogeneous
  PNEs, guiding regulatory policies and practical strategies for competitive ML marketplaces.
