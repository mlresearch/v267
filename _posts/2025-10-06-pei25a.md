title: 'Non-Stationary Predictions May Be More Informative: Exploring Pseudo-Labels
  with a Two-Phase Pattern of Training Dynamics'

abstract: 'Pseudo-labeling is a widely used strategy in semi-supervised learning.
  Existing methods typically select predicted labels with high confidence scores and
  high training stationarity, as pseudo-labels to augment training sets. In contrast,
  this paper explores the pseudo-labeling potential of predicted labels that <b>do
  not</b> exhibit these characteristics. We discover a new type of predicted labels
  suitable for pseudo-labeling, termed <em>two-phase labels</em>, which exhibit a
  two-phase pattern during training: <em>they are initially predicted as one category
  in early training stages and switch to another category in subsequent epochs.</em>
  Case studies show the two-phase labels are informative for decision boundaries.
  To effectively identify the two-phase labels, we design a 2-<em>phasic</em> metric
  that mathematically characterizes their spatial and temporal patterns. Furthermore,
  we propose a loss function tailored for two-phase pseudo-labeling learning, allowing
  models not only to learn correct correlations but also to eliminate false ones.
  Extensive experiments on eight datasets show that <b>our proposed 2-<em>phasic</em>
  metric acts as a powerful booster</b> for existing pseudo-labeling methods by additionally
  incorporating the two-phase labels, achieving an average classification accuracy
  gain of 1.73% on image datasets and 1.92% on graph datasets.'
