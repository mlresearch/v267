abstract: 'Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) has become a critical
  tool for addressing complex real-world problems. However, off-policy MARL methods,
  which rely on joint Q-functions, face significant scalability challenges due to
  the exponentially growing joint action space. In this work, we highlight a critical
  yet often overlooked issue: erroneous Q-target estimation, primarily caused by extrapolation
  error. Our analysis reveals that this error becomes increasingly severe as the number
  of agents grows, leading to unique challenges in MARL due to its expansive joint
  action space and the decentralized execution paradigm. To address these challenges,
  we propose a suite of techniques tailored for off-policy MARL, including annealed
  multi-step bootstrapping, averaged Q-targets, and restricted action representation.
  Experimental results demonstrate that these methods effectively mitigate erroneous
  estimations, yielding substantial performance improvements in challenging benchmarks
  such as SMAC, SMACv2, and Google Research Football.'
