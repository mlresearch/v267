abstract: Current video-language models struggle with long-video understanding due
  to limited context lengths and reliance on sparse frame subsampling, which often
  leads to information loss. In this paper, we introduce $\infty$-Video, which is
  able to process arbitrarily long videos through a continuous-time long-term memory
  (LTM) consolidation mechanism. Our framework augments video Q-formers by making
  them able to process unbounded video contexts efficiently and without requiring
  additional training. Through continuous attention, our approach dynamically allocates
  higher granularity to the most relevant video segments, forming "sticky" memories
  which evolve over time. Experiments with Video-LLaMA and VideoChat2 demonstrate
  improved performance in video question-answering tasks, showcasing the potential
  of continuous-time LTM mechanisms to enable scalable and training-free comprehension
  of long videos.
