title: Communicating Activations Between Language Model Agents
openreview: W6RPXUUFic
abstract: 'Communication between multiple language model (LM) agents has been shown
  to scale up the reasoning ability of LMs. While natural language has been the dominant
  medium for inter-LM communication, it is not obvious this should be the standard:
  not only does natural language communication incur high inference costs that scale
  quickly with the number of both agents and messages, but also the decoding process
  abstracts away too much rich information that could be otherwise accessed from the
  internal activations. In this work, we propose a simple technique whereby LMs communicate
  via <em>activations</em>; concretely, we pause an LM $B$’s computation at an intermediate
  layer, combine its current activation with another LM $A$’s intermediate activation
  via some function $f$, then pass $f$’s output into the next layer of $B$ and continue
  the forward pass till decoding is complete. This approach scales up LMs on new tasks
  with <em>zero</em> additional parameters and data, and saves a <em>substantial amount
  of compute</em> over natural language communication. We test our method with various
  functional forms $f$ on two experimental setups—multi-player coordination games
  and reasoning benchmarks—and find that it achieves up to $27$% improvement over
  natural language communication across datasets with $<$$1/4$ the compute, illustrating
  the superiority and robustness of activations as an alternative "language" for communication
  between LMs.'
