abstract: As machine learning-based prediction systems are increasingly used in high-stakes
  situations, it is important to understand how such predictive models will perform
  upon deployment. Distribution-free uncertainty quantification techniques such as
  conformal prediction provide guarantees about the loss black-box models will incur
  even when the details of the models are hidden. However, such methods are based
  on frequentist probability, which unduly limits their applicability. We revisit
  the central aspects of conformal prediction from a Bayesian perspective and thereby
  illuminate the shortcomings of frequentist guarantees. We propose a practical alternative
  based on Bayesian quadrature that provides interpretable guarantees and offers a
  richer representation of the likely range of losses to be observed at test time.
