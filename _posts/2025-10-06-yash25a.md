title: Near Optimal Best Arm Identification for Clustered Bandits
openreview: 3Jr5Al16MS
abstract: 'This work investigates the problem of best arm identification for multi-agent
  multi-armed bandits. We consider $N$ agents grouped into $M$ clusters, where each
  cluster solves a stochastic bandit problem. The mapping between agents and bandits
  is <em>a priori</em> unknown. Each bandit is associated with $K$ arms, and the goal
  is to identify the best arm for each agent under a $\delta$-probably correct ($\delta$-PC)
  framework, while minimizing sample complexity and communication overhead. We propose
  two novel algorithms: <em>Clustering then Best Arm Identification</em> (Cl-BAI)
  and <em>Best Arm Identification then Clustering</em> (BAI-Cl). Cl-BAI employs a
  two-phase approach that first clusters agents based on the bandit problems they
  are learning, followed by identifying the best arm for each cluster. BAI-Cl reverses
  the sequence by identifying the best arms first and then clustering agents accordingly.
  Both algorithms exploit the successive elimination framework to ensure computational
  efficiency and high accuracy. Theoretical analysis establishes $\delta$-PC guarantees
  for both methods, derives bounds on their sample complexity, and provides a lower
  bound for the problem class. Moreover, when $M$ is small (a constant), we show that
  the sample complexity of (a variant of) BAI-Cl is (order-wise) minimax optimal.
  Experiments on synthetic and real-world (Movie Lens, Yelp) data demonstrates the
  superior performance of the proposed algorithms in terms of sample and communication
  efficiency, particularly in settings where $M \ll N$.'
