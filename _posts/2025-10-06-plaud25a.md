abstract: Hierarchical classification offers an approach to incorporate the concept
  of mistake severity by leveraging a structured, labeled hierarchy. However, decoding
  in such settings frequently relies on heuristic decision rules, which may not align
  with task-specific evaluation metrics. In this work, we propose a framework for
  the optimal decoding of an output probability distribution with respect to a target
  metric. We derive optimal decision rules for increasingly complex prediction settings,
  providing universal algorithms when candidates are limited to the set of nodes.
  In the most general case of predicting a <em>subset of nodes</em>, we focus on rules
  dedicated to the hierarchical $\mathrm{hF}_{\beta}$ scores, tailored to hierarchical
  settings. To demonstrate the practical utility of our approach, we conduct extensive
  empirical evaluations, showcasing the superiority of our proposed optimal strategies,
  particularly in underdetermined scenarios. These results highlight the potential
  of our methods to enhance the performance and reliability of hierarchical classifiers
  in real-world applications.
