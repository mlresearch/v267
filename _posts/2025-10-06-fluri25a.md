title: 'The Perils of Optimizing Learned Reward Functions: Low Training Error Does
  Not Guarantee Low Regret'
openreview: SkYBAXPUBw
abstract: In reinforcement learning, specifying reward functions that capture the
  intended task can be very challenging. Reward learning aims to address this issue
  by <em>learning</em> the reward function. However, a learned reward model may have
  a low error on the data distribution, and yet subsequently produce a policy with
  large regret. We say that such a reward model has an <em>error-regret mismatch</em>.
  The main source of an error-regret mismatch is the distributional shift that commonly
  occurs during policy optimization. In this paper, we mathematically show that a
  sufficiently low expected test error of the reward model guarantees low worst-case
  regret, but that for any <em>fixed</em> expected test error, there exist realistic
  data distributions that allow for error-regret mismatch to occur. We then show that
  similar problems persist even when using policy regularization techniques, commonly
  employed in methods such as RLHF. We hope our results stimulate the theoretical
  and empirical study of improved methods to learn reward models, and better ways
  to measure their quality reliably.
