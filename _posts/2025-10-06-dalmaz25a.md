title: Efficient Noise Calculation in Deep Learning-based MRI Reconstructions
openreview: br7fTbnd16
abstract: Accelerated MRI reconstruction involves solving an ill-posed inverse problem
  where noise in acquired data propagates to the reconstructed images. Noise analyses
  are central to MRI reconstruction for providing an explicit measure of solution
  fidelity and for guiding the design and deployment of novel reconstruction methods.
  However, deep learning (DL)-based reconstruction methods have often overlooked noise
  propagation due to inherent analytical and computational challenges, despite its
  critical importance. This work proposes a theoretically grounded, memory-efficient
  technique to calculate <em>voxel-wise variance</em> for quantifying uncertainty
  due to acquisition noise in accelerated MRI reconstructions. Our approach is based
  on approximating the noise covariance using the DL network’s Jacobian, which is
  intractable to calculate. To circumvent this, we derive an <em>unbiased estimator</em>
  for the diagonal of this covariance matrix—voxel-wise variance—, and introduce a
  Jacobian sketching technique to efficiently implement it. We evaluate our method
  on knee and brain MRI datasets for both data-driven and physics-driven networks
  trained in supervised and unsupervised manners. Compared to empirical references
  obtained via Monte-Carlo simulations, our technique achieves near-equivalent performance
  while reducing computational and memory demands by an order of magnitude or more.
  Furthermore, our method is robust across varying input noise levels, acceleration
  factors, and diverse undersampling schemes, highlighting its broad applicability.
  Our work <em>reintroduces</em> accurate and efficient noise analysis as a central
  tenet of reconstruction algorithms, holding promise to reshape how we evaluate and
  deploy DL-based MRI.
