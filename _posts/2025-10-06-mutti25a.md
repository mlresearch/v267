title: A Classification View on Meta Learning Bandits

abstract: 'Contextual multi-armed bandits are a popular choice to model sequential
  decision-making. <em>E.g.</em>, in a healthcare application we may perform various
  tests to asses a patient condition (exploration) and then decide on the best treatment
  to give (exploitation). When human design strategies, they aim for the exploration
  to be <em>fast</em>, since the patient’s health is at stake, and easy to <em>interpret</em>
  for a physician overseeing the process. However, common bandit algorithms are nothing
  like that: The regret caused by exploration scales with $\sqrt{H}$ over $H$ rounds
  and decision strategies are based on opaque statistical considerations. In this
  paper, we use an original <em>classification view</em> to meta learn interpretable
  and fast exploration plans for a fixed collection of bandits $\mathbb{M}$. The plan
  is prescribed by an interpretable <em>decision tree</em> probing decisions’ payoff
  to classify the test bandit. The test regret of the plan in the <em>stochastic</em>
  and <em>contextual</em> setting scales with $O (\lambda^{-2} C_{\lambda} (\mathbb{M})
  \log^2 (MH))$, being $M$ the size of $\mathbb{M}$, $\lambda$ a separation parameter
  over the bandits, and $C_\lambda (\mathbb{M})$ a novel <em>classification-coefficient</em>
  that fundamentally links meta learning bandits with classification. Through a nearly
  matching lower bound, we show that $C_\lambda (\mathbb{M})$ inherently captures
  the complexity of the setting.'
