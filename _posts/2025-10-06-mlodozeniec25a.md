abstract: Causal inference is a key research area in machine learning, yet confusion
  reigns over the tools needed to tackle it. There are prevalent claims in the machine
  learning literature that you need a bespoke causal framework or notation to answer
  causal questions. In this paper, we make it clear that you can answer any causal
  inference question within the realm of probabilistic modelling and inference, without
  causal-specific tools or notation. Through concrete examples, we demonstrate how
  causal questions can be tackled by writing down the probability of everything. We
  argue for the advantages of the generality of the probabilistic modelling lens,
  when compared to bespoke causal frameworks. Lastly, we reinterpret causal tools
  as emerging from standard probabilistic modelling and inference, elucidating their
  necessity and utility.
