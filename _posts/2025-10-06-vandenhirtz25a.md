abstract: Understanding the decision-making process of machine learning models provides
  valuable insights into the task, the data, and the reasons behind a model’s failures.
  In this work, we propose a method that performs inherently interpretable predictions
  through the instance-wise sparsification of input images. To align the sparsification
  with human perception, we learn the masking in the space of semantically meaningful
  pixel regions rather than on pixel-level. Additionally, we introduce an explicit
  way to dynamically determine the required level of sparsity for each instance. We
  show empirically on semi-synthetic and natural image datasets that our inherently
  interpretable classifier produces more meaningful, human-understandable predictions
  than state-of-the-art benchmarks.
