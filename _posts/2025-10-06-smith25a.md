title: 'Functional Alignment Can Mislead: Examining Model Stitching'

abstract: A common belief in the representational comparison literature is that if
  two representations can be functionally aligned, they must capture similar information.
  In this paper we focus on model stitching and show that models can be functionally
  aligned, but represent very different information. Firstly, we show that discriminative
  models with very different biases can be stitched together. We then show that models
  trained to solve entirely different tasks on different data modalities, and even
  clustered random noise, can be successfully stitched into MNIST or ImageNet-trained
  models. We end with a discussion of the wider impact of our results on the community’s
  current beliefs. Overall, our paper draws attention to the need to correctly interpret
  the results of such functional similarity measures and highlights the need for approaches
  that capture informational similarity.
