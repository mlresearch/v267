title: 'SyncMind: Measuring Agent Out-of-Sync Recovery in Collaborative Software Engineering'
openreview: 6TDSDdgP7Z
abstract: 'Software engineering (SE) is increasingly collaborative, with developers
  working together on shared complex codebases. Effective collaboration in shared
  environments requires participants—whether humans or AI agents—to stay on the same
  page as their environment evolves. When a collaborator’s understanding diverges
  from the current state—what we term the <em>out-of-sync</em> challenge—the collaborator’s
  actions may fail, leading to integration issues. In this work, we introduce <b>SyncMind</b>,
  a framework that systematically defines the <em>out-of-sync</em> problem faced by
  large language model (LLM) agents in collaborative software engineering (CSE). Based
  on <b><em>SyncMind</em></b>, we create <b>SyncBench</b>, a benchmark featuring 24,332
  instances of agent <em>out-of-sync</em> scenarios in real-world CSE derived from
  21 popular <em>GitHub</em> repositories with executable verification tests. Experiments
  on <b><em>SyncBench</em></b> uncover critical insights into existing LLM agents’
  capabilities and limitations. Besides substantial performance gaps among agents
  (from <em>Llama-3.1</em> agents $\leq 3.33%$ to <em>Claude-3.5-Sonnet</em> $\geq
  28.18%$), their consistently low collaboration willingness ($\le 4.86%$) suggests
  fundamental limitations of existing LLM in CSE. However, when collaboration occurs,
  it positively correlates with <em>out-of-sync</em> recovery success. Minimal performance
  differences in agents’ resource-aware <em>out-of-sync</em> recoveries further reveal
  their significant lack of resource awareness and adaptability, shedding light on
  future development of resource-efficient collaborative systems. Our code and data
  are openly available on our project website: https://xhguo7.github.io/SyncMind/.'
